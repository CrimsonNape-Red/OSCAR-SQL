Let's get your first night into OSCAR

  1. Check your preferencesSome options in File › Preferences affect how data is imported. Some are set for you when a ResMed device is detected.
  2. Insert your SD cardUse a card reader, then choose Import CPAP data above. OSCAR finds the card for you.
  3. Give it a few minutesThe first import reads your whole history and can take a few minutes. Later imports are quick.

Design notes

What changes from the current page

  • The actions become compact tiles with simple line icons instead of screenshot thumbnails. Import is the primary action.
  • An Insights tile and tab, after Statistics, reserve a place for a possible new page.
  • The paragraph of sentences about the last night becomes a grid of cards that can be scanned at a glance.
  • Each card compares the night with your 7-day and 30-day averages and shows the last 30 nights as a small trend line.
  • Clicking a card opens the Daily view for that night at the matching graph.
  • If the newest data is more than a day old, a notice offers to import.
  • On a new profile, a three-step getting-started panel replaces the cards.

How the comparison colours work

  • The arrow shows direction: ▲ the night was higher than the average, ▼ lower.
  • The colour shows meaning: green is better, red is worse. Lower is better for AHI, events, leaks and flow limitation; higher is better for time used.
  • Pressure and session count are grey because higher or lower is neither good nor bad.
  • Averages cover the nights before the one shown, matching how the current page calculates its 7-day AHI and leak averages.

Card colour options

  • Plain: red and green appear only in the comparisons.
  • By group: a light tint shows the kind of number: breathing, mask and pressure, or usage.
  • By status: a card is tinted green or red only when both comparisons agree it is better or worse.

Device-dependent content

  • The pressure card wording depends on the therapy mode, as it does today: a constant pressure for CPAP, "under X for 95% of the time" for APAP, an EPAP–IPAP pair for bilevel and ASV modes.
  • AHI can show RDI instead when that preference is set.
  • Cards for data a device doesn't record (flow limitation, large leaks) are left out. The grid is open-ended, so more cards can be added later.
  • Event colours come from the user's channel colour settings.
  • When there is oximetry data for the same night, it will need its own card. Its design is still to be decided.

Typography

  • Numbers and headings: Lexend. Body text: Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for low-vision readers.
  • Both are under the SIL Open Font License, so they can be bundled with OSCAR and look the same on every platform.
